Beaverbrook Art Gallery

703 Queen St · opened 1959, Lord Beaverbrook's gift · Neil Stewart's 1959 original, the 2017 MacKay-Lyons Sweetapple stone-box pavilion, and KPMB's curved Harrison McCain Pavilion (2022). Building 108.5 m long · exterior 5,467 tris, interior 11,442 tris. First pass: the art inside is stand-in canvases — the real collection stays the gallery's.

Charlotte Street Arts Centre

732 Charlotte St · built 1884–85 as Charlotte Street School (Dumaresq & Mott, Italianate red brick, bellcast-roof cupola — cousin to their Legislature dome), restored 2005 as the city's arts centre. The Interior view stands at the back of the 1917 annex's top-floor auditorium, looking to the stage. 31.5 × 45.0 m, 22.7 m to the finial · exterior 12,172 tris, interior 11,500 tris. First pass.

Legislative Assembly Building

706 Queen St · opened 1882, J. C. Dumaresq's Second Empire seat of the Legislative Assembly — Dorchester sandstone, corner towers, Britannia on the roof, 41 m to the top of the dome. The Interior view stands on the chamber floor looking down the green desks to the Speaker's chair. 45.0 × 48.6 m · exterior 13,493 tris, interior 15,366 tris. First pass.

The maps

MVT Fredericton isn't drawn from imagination. The city in the game is baked from New Brunswick's own open survey data — the provincial road network, province-wide LiDAR, aerial orthophotos and measured river soundings — catalogued source by source in the Maps of New Brunswick project. Here is that same data, live:

Downtown Fredericton in 3D, on GeoNB's own imagery: every building stands at its LiDAR-measured height. Drag to look around; zoom with ctrl-scroll or pinch (the page keeps plain scrolling).

🛣️ Roads — the province's own network

The streets are the New Brunswick Road Network (NBRN), the province's authoritative road layer, published through GeoNB. Each segment carries its real name, lane count, speed limit and traffic-flow direction — the same attributes the game's traffic drives on and its routing graph is built from, so Queen Street is Queen Street, one-way where the real one is.

🏙️ Buildings & ground — one LiDAR survey

New Brunswick is covered by province-wide LiDAR. The game's buildings are the LiDAR-derived footprints GeoNB publishes, each with a measured height — roof elevation minus ground elevation — and the terrain under them comes from the same survey. The 3D view above extrudes exactly those measured heights; nothing is eyeballed to look right.

🛰️ The imagery — 15 cm orthophotos

The aerial imagery is GeoNB's orthophoto programme: the latest contribution covers 13,000 km² at 0.15 m per pixel, flown in the summer of 2025 — sharp enough to read road markings and driveways. It's the ground truth the game world is checked against, and it's the basemap under the map above.

🌊 The riverbed — measured soundings

The Saint John River (Wolastoq) has a real bottom. Its bed through the Fredericton reach is sampled from the Canadian Hydrographic Service's NONNA bathymetric data — measured soundings gridded along transects through the city, mostly 2–8 m deep in the channel — so the water the game dives and fishes in has the shape the surveyors measured, not a flat blue plane.

🌾 Wetlands & water

GeoNB's regulated wetlands inventory marks the marshes and bogs — in the game they're the soft, slow ground at the city's edges. The river channel itself comes from the provincial hydrographic network (NBHN), which is a different thing from a wetland, and the game treats them differently.

🌲 The trees — canopy from LiDAR, species from inventories

Tree positions and heights come from a canopy-height model built on GeoNB's 2024 LiDAR — the canopy the lasers actually measured, at 2 m resolution. Species come from inventories, not guesses: the City of Fredericton's tree inventory records 21,186 city-managed trees by species (Norway maple, sugar maple, red oak and green ash lead the count), which is why autumn colour in the game varies species by species — maples red, oaks russet, ash yellow. Beyond the city-managed trees, the provincial forest inventory (GeoNB DNR Forest) gives the woodlots their species mix and stand heights, so a pine stand is pine and a maple stand is maple.
